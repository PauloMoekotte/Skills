# Glossary — AI Playbook for Inclusive and Accessible Cities

- **Accessibility** — an international human right and precondition for inclusion: the extent to which products, systems, services, and environments can be used by people with the widest range of characteristics and capabilities (Ch 1)
- **Accessibility review** — a required check that every technology product, including AI, is usable by people with physical, sensory, and cognitive disabilities (Ch 4)
- **Accessibility safari** — in-person field validation where teams physically measure real-world conditions and compare them against AI output (Ch 5)
- **Accessible technology** — products and services thoroughly tested to ensure usability for individuals with disabilities (Glossary)
- **AI Register** — a public, plain-language inventory of AI systems in use, describing what each does, its data, oversight, and risk handling (Ch 4, 8)
- **Algorithm** — a step-by-step set of instructions a computer follows to solve a problem or perform a task (Glossary)
- **Algorithmic bias** — unfair or discriminatory outcomes when algorithms favor or disadvantage certain groups due to training data, design, or policies (Ch 1, 3)
- **Amsterdam For All** — the initiative promoting inclusivity and accessibility in the City of Amsterdam, launched 2020 with World Enabled (Ch 7)
- **Anomaly detection** — identifying items that deviate from a pattern, e.g. wheelchair-accessible transit updates or fall detection (Ch 1)
- **Anticipatory (predictive) AI** — predicts future needs based on historical data, e.g. traffic patterns, infrastructure failure (Ch 1)
- **Artificial intelligence** — information-processing technologies that integrate models and algorithms to learn and perform cognitive tasks leading to prediction and decision-making (UNESCO definition) (Ch 1)
- **Asset inventory** — cataloguing existing policies, commitments, grants, bodies, and staff that support inclusion before starting new work (Ch 3)
- **Assistive technology** — hardware or software added to or incorporated in a system that increases accessibility for an individual user (Ch 1)
- **Bug bashing** — asking people who have never used the technology to use it and record every issue as a "bug" for the team to fix (Ch 5)
- **Cities for All Global Compact and Campaign** — international effort ensuring cities are designed and operated to be inclusive for all, especially people with disabilities and older persons (Ch 2, 7)
- **Civil rights protection** — safeguards so AI is not used in ways that infringe rights in profiling, surveillance, law enforcement, or courts (Ch 8)
- **Clustering** — grouping similar objects for efficient planning (economies of scale) (Ch 1)
- **Compensation for participation** — paying community stakeholders and user testers for their lived experience and expertise (Ch 3)
- **CRPD (UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, 2006)** — international human rights treaty; Article 9 covers equal access to physical environment, transport, and information; Article 20 covers personal mobility (Ch 8)
- **Data anonymization** — techniques that protect individuals' identities while still enabling valuable AI applications (Ch 8)
- **Data inventory** — reviewing existing technology and datasets available for tool development, monitoring, and evaluation (Ch 3)
- **Data labeling** — tagging objects in datasets so AI can learn to identify them; quality depends on labeling accuracy (Ch 5)
- **Data sharing agreement** — the mechanism for using data held by a different city department or partner (Ch 3)
- **Decisive AI** — automates complex decision-making and provides recommendations based on patterns, e.g. resource allocation, infrastructure decisions (Ch 1)
- **Detection & classification** — identifying, categorizing, and translating information including images, numbers, and sound (Ch 1)
- **Digital inclusion** — ensuring all individuals have equal access to, and ability to use, digital technologies and the internet (Ch 1)
- **Digital redlining** — algorithms systematically excluding groups based on income, location, or other factors, akin to historical housing redlining (Ch 1)
- **Digital transformation** — integrating digital technologies into all areas of an organization or city, fundamentally changing operations and value delivery (Glossary)
- **Equity** — providing different levels of support based on individual needs to ensure fair outcomes, lifting up marginalized communities (Glossary)
- **Ethical Leaflet** — Amsterdam's tool for defining moral motivations and implications before a project, via questions like "Why are we doing this?" and "For whom?" (Ch 2)
- **EU AI Act (2023)** — EU regulation tiering AI by risk (minimal, limited, high-risk, unacceptable), applying to all AI deployed in the EU regardless of provider (Ch 8)
- **Fairness metrics** — quantitative checks that algorithm outcomes are equitable across demographic or geographic groups (Ch 8)
- **Forecasting & prediction** — estimating the next value in a series from training data, e.g. asset failure, disasters (Ch 1)
- **Garbage in, garbage out** — if training data is not diverse and representative, AI reproduces stereotypes and bias (Ch 3)
- **Generation** — creating new data or content, e.g. chatbots helping residents navigate city services (Ch 1)
- **Generative AI** — creates new content (text, images, music); treat as not yet mature for critical public infrastructure (Ch 1, 8)
- **Governance** — the processes, structures, and traditions determining how power is exercised and how decisions are made (Glossary)
- **Human-centered design** — a design approach prioritizing users' needs, preferences, and contexts for meaningful experiences (Glossary)
- **Inclusion** — ensuring all people can participate in public life regardless of physical, cognitive, and sensory abilities (Glossary)
- **KPI for inclusion** — project metrics measuring fairness, accuracy, and quality of service delivery (Ch 3, 6)
- **Lived experience** — the direct expertise people with disabilities and older persons hold about barriers they face; core input for needs assessment (Ch 3)
- **LLM (large language model)** — an AI system trained on vast text data that can generate natural language (Glossary)
- **Nothing about us without us** — the disability rights motto and standing test for whether affected communities were genuinely included (Ch 2)
- **Open by default** — sharing technical resources (documents, code) publicly unless privacy gives a compelling reason not to (Ch 4)
- **Optimization** — producing outputs that optimize for a set of outcomes, e.g. prioritizing infrastructure by cost, inclusivity, equity (Ch 1)
- **Outcomes assessment** — scoring pilot results on accuracy and fairness (Ch 5)
- **People with disabilities** — individuals with acute, temporary, or permanent physical, cognitive, or sensory impairments; includes older persons with functional disabilities (Glossary)
- **PETs (privacy-enhancing technologies)** — techniques protecting individuals' privacy without compromising AI efficacy (Ch 8)
- **Pre-mortem** — reviewers imagine the project has already failed and enumerate why, surfacing weaknesses fixable before execution (Ch 4)
- **Proactive transparency** — explaining how AI works in clear, non-technical language, especially when it makes decisions (Ch 4)
- **Prototype / pilot** — a small-scale test of the technology: a model of what it will do, or a live test in one area or for one issue (Ch 5)
- **Red teaming** — testers intentionally try to produce errors and harmful outcomes, stress-testing for results that hinder inclusivity (Ch 5)
- **Stakeholders** — individuals, groups, or organizations with an interest in the project and outcome, often including people with lived experience (Glossary)
- **Synthetic data** — generated data used as a privacy-preserving alternative to real personal data (Ch 8)
- **Tada Manifesto** — Amsterdam's six principles for a digital city: inclusivity, control, tailored to the people, legitimacy, openness, transparency (Ch 2)
- **Ten Step Framework** — the playbook's central process for developing inclusive AI, from stakeholder values to monitoring and evaluation (Ch 2)
- **Training data** — large, diverse sets of information used to teach AI models to learn patterns and make predictions (Ch 3)
- **Transparency and accountability** — explaining how technology works to non-technical audiences and taking responsibility, especially when AI replaces human decision-makers (Ch 2, 8)
- **Universal Design** — designing products, environments, and services usable and inclusive for all people regardless of age, ability, or social status (Ch 1)
- **Urban planning** — designing and shaping the physical layout and organization of cities and towns (Glossary)
- **User** — a person who interacts with the product, service, or environment (Glossary)
- **User-Centered Design** — developing solutions with strong focus on end users, engaging people with disabilities in design and testing (Ch 2)
- **WCAG 2.0** — Web Content Accessibility Guidelines; the standard for accessible digital content and services (Ch 8)
- **3D point cloud** — laser-scanner data capturing every point in a street scene, used to record sidewalk widths and obstacles (Ch 4)
