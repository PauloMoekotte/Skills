# Glossary

**Accountability** — chain of human responsibility across the AI life cycle, incl. answerability, auditability and liability (Ch 5).

**Agentic AI** — autonomous AI systems that decide and act with minimal human intervention, using tools to achieve objectives (Ch 2).

**AI winter** — period of waning interest/investment after waves of AI hype (Ch 2).

**AI community of practice** — cross-government forum (monthly meetings) for those working on AI in the public sector (Ch 3).

**AI systems inventory** — live register of all deployed AI/ML systems, their purpose, risks, ownership and dates (Ch 9).

**Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS)** — mandatory standard for central departments and in-scope arm's length bodies to document algorithmic tools used in decision-making (Ch 1, 5).

**Answerability** — establishing who is responsible at each stage of the AI life cycle, incl. supply chain (Ch 5).

**Auditability** — documenting every stage of the AI life cycle in an accessible, traceable way (Ch 5).

**Agentic/reasoning framework (ReAct)** — LLM pattern where the model reasons about how to perform a task, then the response automates service calls; requires human review of actions (Ch 8).

**Biometric data** — personal data from physical/physiological/behavioural characteristics confirming unique identification; special category when processed for identification (Ch 7).

**Computer vision (CV)** — AI field enabling computers to interpret and analyse images/video (object recognition, facial recognition, scene understanding) (Ch 2).

**Contestability and redress** — mechanisms to challenge AI systems/outputs and seek remedy, designed before deployment (Ch 5).

**Data leakage** — AI model responses revealing confidential information, e.g. personal data (Ch 8).

**Data minimisation** — process only the minimum personal data needed; less data = less risk (Ch 7).

**Data protection by design** — integrating data protection safeguards into processing throughout the life cycle (Ch 7).

**Data/model poisoning** — tampering with training data so the model produces incorrect or harmful output (Ch 8).

**Deep learning (DL)** — subset of ML with complex neural architectures for complex tasks and large data (Ch 2).

**DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment)** — assessment of data protection/privacy risk and mitigations; mandatory in listed AI scenarios (Ch 7).

**Dynamic Purchasing System (DPS)** — procurement route open to new supplier registrations at any time; further competition only (Ch 4).

**Embeddings** — numerical representations of text processed by ML models (Ch 2).

**Equality Act 2010** — legislation giving public bodies obligations (protected characteristics) relevant to AI fairness (Ch 5, 6).

**Ethics committee** — specialised governance body assessing ethical implications of AI (fairness, transparency, privacy) (Ch 9).

**Five-part business case (Green Book)** — Treasury guidance business case, mandatory near/above ~£10M investment (Ch 4).

**Framework agreement** — procurement route where suppliers are awarded at launch; closed to new registrations (Ch 4).

**Generative AI** — subset of AI generating text, images, video via probabilistic models; strong at plausible content, weak at facts (Ch 2).

**Hallucination (confabulation)** — plausible-but-false output from probabilistic models (Ch 2, 8).

**Human-in-the-loop** — human oversight/validation of AI outputs, especially high-impact decisions (Ch 1, 7).

**Insecure AI tool chain** — tools used to train/host/serialise/operate AI models lacking basic security (Ch 8).

**Intellectual property rights (IPR)** — ownership and use rights of IP generated by/with AI; clarify early in contracts (Ch 4, 6).

**Justified trust** — public trust earned by competent, responsible, ethical AI development (Ch 5).

**Liability** — ensuring all parties act lawfully and understand their obligations; vendor responsibilities contractually defined (Ch 5).

**Machine learning (ML)** — branch of AI that learns from data by extracting features and relationships (Ch 2).

**Meaningful human control** — human validation of high-risk AI-influenced decisions; for instant apps, control shifts to testing, monitoring, redress (Ch 1).

**Model bias** — innate deviation in a model giving error between predicted and actual values (Ch 2).

**Model drift** — divergence between current operating conditions and the training period, requiring retraining or replacement (Ch 9).

**Natural language processing (NLP)** — AI field processing human language (translation, classification, sentiment, conversational AI) (Ch 2).

**Neural network (NN)** — computational model inspired by the brain; learns by adjusting weights between nodes during training cycles (Ch 2).

**Open Government Licence v3.0** — licence under which this playbook is published (metadata).

**PETs (privacy-enhancing technologies)** — tools for anonymisation, synthetic data, collaborative analysis without disclosing data copies (Ch 7).

**Perturbation attack** — stealthy modification of AI inputs to get a desired response (Ch 8).

**Phishing (AI-amplified)** — generative AI crafting convincing, personalised phishing at scale (Ch 8).

**PPN 02/24** — Procurement Policy Note with optional questions on suppliers' AI use transparency (Ch 4).

**Procurement Act 2023** — legislation (in force 2025) governing public procurement alongside Public Contract Regulations 2015 (Ch 4).

**Prompt injection** — crafted prompts circumventing system instructions; direct (from the user) or indirect (via other data) (Ch 8).

**Public Contract Regulations 2015** — primary public procurement legislation (Ch 4).

**Public Sector Contract (PSC)** — contract with provisions on IPR, data protection, equality, diversity and human rights (Ch 4).

**RAG (retrieval augmented generation)** — in-context learning where a search of permitted data augments the model; preserves access controls, susceptible to indirect prompt injection (Ch 8, 10).

**Representational bias** — people under/over/misrepresented in training data leading to harmful stereotypes or performance disparities (Ch 5).

**RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback)** — training method where humans rank outputs (Ch 3).

**Robustness** — maintaining performance/stability under different conditions; reliability is a core component (Ch 5).

**Secure by Design principles** — CDDO-developed security baseline required for government services (Ch 1, 8).

**Senior Responsible Owner (SRO)** — the named individual accountable for AI use in a project (Ch 1, 5).

**Speech recognition (SR/ASR)** — ML field processing speech; speech-to-text and speech-to-speech (Ch 2).

**Supervised learning** — ML trained on labelled data with correct answers (Ch 2).

**Synthetic data** — artificial data mirroring statistical aspects of a real set without real individuals (Ch 7, 10).

**Transparency (5 types)** — technical, process, outcome-based, internal, public (Ch 5).

**Unsupervised learning** — ML that identifies patterns/groupings in unlabelled data (Ch 2).

**Use cases register** — way of capturing and prioritising AI use cases by feasibility and value (Ch 3).

**Validation** — objective evidence that requirements for a specific intended use are fulfilled (ISO 9000:2015) (Ch 9).

**Vendor lock-in** — dependency on a single supplier that inhibits transfer; design out via specification (Ch 4).

**White paper: A pro-innovation approach to AI regulation** — sets out 5 principles for responsible AI innovation across sectors (Ch 1, 5).
