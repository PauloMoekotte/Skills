# Chapter 4: Buying AI

## Core Idea
Buying AI well means writing a sound business case, following existing procurement guidance, choosing the right route to market, specifying requirements that cover data/bias/liability, and aligning procurement with ethics — with commercial colleagues engaged from the start.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Business case selection**: near/above ~£10M investment → Treasury Green Book five-part business case (mandatory); below → strongly consider CDDO agile business case guidance. Mandatory assurance for non-BAU spend: >£100K for digital activities, >£1M for technology activities.
- **Framework vs DPS decision table**: pick a framework when you want pre-vetted suppliers and low buyer effort; pick a Dynamic Purchasing System (DPS) in a fast-moving market where new suppliers need to join at any time.

| | Framework | DPS |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier access | Awarded at launch; closed to new registrations (primes can add subcontractors) | Open for new registrations at any time |
| Structure | Often divided into lots by product/service type | Suppliers filterable by categories |
| Compliance | Thorough ongoing CCS checks; buyer does less at call-off (excluding G-Cloud) | Basic checks by CCS; buyer completes the rest at call-off |
| Buying options | Various, incl. direct award | Further competition only |

- **Routes to market**: framework/DPS agreements (CCS), Find a Tender service procurement (bespoke requirements), and MoUs with suppliers (preferential pricing, accessible via any route). Always comply with the Public Contract Regulations 2015 and the Procurement Act 2023 (in force 2025).

## Key Concepts
- **Guidelines for AI procurement**: best practice across preparation, publication, selection, evaluation & award, and contract implementation & management.
- **Digital, Data and Technology Playbook**: sourcing and contracting guidance on a "comply or explain" basis for central departments; includes AI/ML and IPR guidance.
- **PPN 02/24**: optional questions to improve transparency of suppliers' AI use in procurements.
- **Data Ethics Framework**: government's framework for appropriate and responsible data use.
- **AI Management Essentials** (DSIT): self-assessment tool distilling AI standards into baseline requirements for suppliers; to be embedded in procurement frameworks.
- **Vendor lock-in**: a risk to be designed out in your specification (data portability, transfer to successor suppliers).

## Mental Models
- Use **"specify for understanding, not just capability"**: requirements must demand transparency into the supplier's AI approach — you must be able to understand, audit and explain the system.
- Use **"ethics in the contract"**: bake data ethics, bias mitigation, diversity, transparency and explainability into requirements and the Public Sector Contract (PSC), which already covers IPR, data protection and EDI.
- Use **"engage commercial early"**: route-to-market decisions and contract terms drive each other; decide them before you're committed.

## Anti-patterns
- **Buying without a business case**: decision makers can't assess return on investment in resources and costs.
- **Treating AI procurement like a commodity buy**: omitting bias/data-quality requirements, data strategy, or IPR terms invites downstream failures.
- **Ignoring emerging-market dynamics**: the AI supply base changes fast; a closed framework may block you from better new suppliers, whereas a DPS stays open.
- **Not specifying dummy data or budget guidance**: suppliers can't test against real data (and shouldn't); hidden costs go unaccounted.

## Worked Example
**Requirement specification checklist.** When drafting an AI procurement, ensure the spec:
- starts with a problem statement
- highlights data strategy and requirements; focuses on data quality, bias mitigation and limitations
- requires understanding of the supplier's AI approach
- includes strategies to avoid vendor lock-in and clarifies IPR on any new software
- applies Data Ethics Framework principles and the data ethics requirements checklist for suppliers
- mentions integration with associated technologies, support/maintenance needs, data formats and dummy data
- gives guidance on budget to cover hidden costs, and states acceptable liabilities/risk appetite
- aligns with the PSC provisions (IPR, data protection, equality/diversity/human rights)

## Key Takeaways
1. Write the business case first; use the Green Book five-part case for large investments, agile cases below, and assure spend above the thresholds.
2. Choose framework vs DPS based on market speed and supplier access needs; MoUs add preferential pricing to any route.
3. Specify requirements that cover data, bias, transparency, lock-in, IPR, dummy data and liabilities — not just features.
4. Align procurement with the Data Ethics Framework and PSC from the outset.
5. Involve commercial colleagues at the very start; the market is evolving faster than policy.
